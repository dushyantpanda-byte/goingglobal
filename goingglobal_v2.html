10 Steps to Export from India

A complete walkthrough from idea to first payment received, in the right order.

1
Register your business as an exporter
IEC code, GST, RCMC — the three mandatory registrations
›

Before your first export shipment, three registrations are mandatory:

  • Importer-Exporter Code (IEC): Issued by DGFT. Apply online at dgft.gov.in. Takes 2–3 working days. Lifetime validity. Fee: ₹500.
  • GST Registration: Required to claim zero-rating on exports and file LUT/bond for exports without payment of IGST. If already GST registered, no fresh registration needed.
  • RCMC (Registration-cum-Membership Certificate): Required for claiming MEIS/RODTEP benefits. Issued by relevant export promotion council (APEDA for agri, EEPC for engineering, etc.). Annual fee varies.
💡 Tip: Apply for all three simultaneously. IEC is instant; RCMC takes 1–2 weeks. Don't wait for one to get the other.
⚠️ Warning: You cannot legally export goods without a valid IEC. Customs will not process your shipping bill without it.
2
Choose your export product and HS code
The right HS code determines your duty drawback, RODTEP rate, and export restrictions
›

Every exported product must be classified under the Harmonised System (HS) of nomenclature — an 8-digit code in India's ITC-HS system.

  • Use the DGFT Trade Portal (tradeservices.dgft.gov.in) to search by product keyword.
  • Your HS code determines: export duty (if any), RODTEP rate, duty drawback rate, and whether a licence or restriction applies.
  • Some products are "Prohibited" (can't export), "Restricted" (need licence), or "Canalised" (only through STC/MMTC).
  • For multi-component products (e.g., machinery), use the rule of "essential character" from HS Chapter Notes.
⚠️ Warning: Wrong HS code declaration on the shipping bill is a customs offence. Get it verified by a customs broker if unsure.
3
Choose your Incoterms and price your export
FOB vs CIF vs DAP — who bears what cost and risk
›

Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) define who — buyer or seller — is responsible for freight, insurance, customs, and delivery risk at each point of the journey.

  • FOB (Free On Board): Most common for Indian exporters. Your responsibility ends when goods are loaded on the ship at origin port. Buyer pays freight and insurance.
  • CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight): You pay freight and insurance to destination port. Higher invoice value → higher RODTEP benefit base.
  • DAP (Delivered At Place): You deliver to buyer's door — maximum risk, but possible with established buyers.
💡 Tip: Indian RBI regulations require that export proceeds must be realised within 9 months of the date of export (15 months for certain countries). FOB is simplest for tracking payment timelines.
4
Open a dedicated export current account and get an EEFC account
Separate your export proceeds — required for FEMA compliance
›

FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act) requires all export proceeds to be received through authorised banking channels.

  • Current account with AD (Authorised Dealer) bank: Mandatory for all exporters. Your export proceeds must be credited here.
  • EEFC Account (Exchange Earners' Foreign Currency): Holds up to 100% of your foreign exchange earnings in USD/EUR/GBP. Use this balance to pay import invoices, travel expenses, or foreign services without conversion.
  • EEFC balance must be used within a reasonable period — RBI has guidelines on minimum utilisation if you hold large balances.
⚠️ FEMA Warning: Accepting payments in Indian rupees from a non-resident account outside India, or through crypto, is non-compliant and attracts penalty up to 3× the forex amount.
5
File a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) for GST-free exports
Export without paying IGST — the single biggest cash flow benefit for SME exporters
›

Under GST, exports are "zero-rated". This means you can export without paying IGST, by filing a LUT (Letter of Undertaking) with your GST jurisdiction.

  • File LUT online at GST portal (gst.gov.in) under Services → User Services → Furnish Letter of Undertaking (LUT).
  • LUT must be filed for each financial year before your first export of that year. One-time filing, valid for the full FY.
  • With LUT: Export without paying IGST, and claim refund of ITC (input tax credit) on inputs used in exported goods.
  • Without LUT: Pay IGST at export and claim refund later — ties up working capital for months.
💡 Tip: File your LUT in the first week of April every year. Processing is instant on the GST portal. Don't forget — penalties for exporting without LUT or bond are significant.
⚠️ Warning: If you are a "defaulter" on any previous GST obligation, you cannot file LUT — you must submit a bond with surety instead.
6
Get export finance: pre-shipment and post-shipment credit
Banks offer concessional rates for export credit — often 2–4% cheaper than regular working capital
›

Export credit is regulated by RBI and offered at concessional interest rates under the Priority Sector Lending framework.

  • Pre-shipment credit (Packing Credit): Working capital loan from the date of receiving purchase order/LC to the date of shipment. Available in INR or foreign currency (PCFC — cheaper as it's linked to LIBOR/SOFR).
  • Post-shipment credit: Finance against documents after shipment. Types include: Negotiation of LC bills, Purchase of DP/DA bills, Export Bill Discounting.
  • EXIM Bank schemes: For medium/long-term export credit, buyer's credit, and overseas investment financing.
💡 Tip: ECGC credit insurance (NIRVIK scheme) allows you to get higher credit limits from banks at lower collateral — because the bank's risk is covered by ECGC up to 90%.
7
Prepare your export documentation
Commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and the shipping bill
›

Incorrect documentation is the #1 reason export shipments get stuck. Every document must be consistent — same value, same description, same quantity.

  • Commercial Invoice: Must mention IEC, GST number, buyer details, HS code, INCOTERMS, and LUT/bond reference number.
  • Packing List: Gross weight, net weight, number of packages, dimensions.
  • Certificate of Origin (CoO): Required by buyer's country customs. Issued by DGFT (Form A for GSP countries), or Chamber of Commerce (general CoO).
  • Shipping Bill: Filed by your CHA (Customs Handling Agent) on ICEGATE portal. This is the primary customs export declaration.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill: Issued by shipping line/airline. Original B/L is a title document — handle carefully.
⚠️ Warning: The description, value, and quantity on your invoice, packing list, and shipping bill must match exactly. Discrepancies cause customs examination, demurrage, and possible penalty.
8
Get paid: choose the right payment instrument
Letter of Credit vs. advance payment vs. DP/DA — matching risk to reward
›

Getting paid for exports is not automatic. Payment terms determine your risk exposure and cost of credit.

  • Advance Payment (T/T advance): Best for you, riskiest for buyer. Common for small first orders and trusted buyers. Bank will give pre-shipment credit against purchase order.
  • Letter of Credit (LC): Bank guarantee from buyer's bank that payment will be made if documents comply. Most secure for large orders. Get it confirmed by your Indian bank for maximum protection.
  • Documents against Payment (DP/Sight): Buyer pays on sight of documents. Moderate risk — buyer could refuse documents.
  • Documents against Acceptance (DA/Usance): Buyer accepts bill and pays in 30/60/90 days. Higher risk — use ECGC cover.
  • Open Account: Ship first, get paid later. Only for very established buyers. Must have ECGC credit insurance.
💡 Razorpay Cross-Border: For digital service exports and e-commerce, Razorpay's payment gateway supports receipt of foreign currency in compliance with FEMA — no banking paperwork needed for payments under $25,000 per transaction.
9
Realise and repatriate your export proceeds
RBI requires proceeds within 9 months — this is the most critical FEMA compliance requirement
›

Under RBI Master Direction on Export of Goods and Services (2016), all export proceeds must be realised and repatriated to India within 9 months from the date of export.

  • Your bank will generate an Export Data Processing and Monitoring System (EDPMS) entry for every shipping bill. Payment must be matched against this entry.
  • If proceeds are not received within 9 months, you must apply to your AD bank for an extension with justification. Bank can extend up to 9+9 = 18 months for exceptional cases.
  • For software/IT service exports, the time limit is 9 months from invoice date.
  • Write-off of unrealised export proceeds is allowed (subject to RBI limits) only after genuine efforts to recover payment.
⚠️ FEMA Warning: Failure to realise proceeds within the permitted period attracts penalty under FEMA Section 13 — minimum penalty is equal to the unpaid amount, with compounding interest. Banks are required to report unrealised proceeds to RBI.

10
Claim your export incentives: RODTEP, Duty Drawback, EPCG
Most SMEs leave 2–5% of export value on the table by not claiming these

Three main incentive schemes are available to almost all exporters:

  • RODTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products): Remission of embedded taxes (state levies, electricity duty, mandi tax, etc.) that aren't refunded through other mechanisms. Rate varies by HS code — check DGFT schedule. Credited as transferable e-scrip in the ICEGATE portal.
  • Duty Drawback: Refund of Customs duty paid on imported inputs used in exported goods. Two schemes: All Industry Rate (AIR — automatic, based on HS code) and Brand Rate (for large volumes, requires calculation of actual duty paid).
  • EPCG (Export Promotion Capital Goods): Import capital goods at 0% customs duty, in exchange for exporting 6× the duty saved over 6 years. Ideal for manufacturers who need to upgrade machinery.
💡 Tip: RODTEP and Duty Drawback are declared at the time of filing the shipping bill. If you forget, you cannot claim them later on the same bill. Make sure your CHA declares both.
›

12 Tips That Save Exporters Money

Practical, proven moves that most first-time exporters miss.

Always declare RODTEP on your shipping bill

RODTEP credit (0.3%–4.3% of FOB value) must be declared by your CHA at the time of filing the shipping bill. You cannot claim it retroactively.

Saves: 0.3%–4.3% of FOB value

File LUT on April 1st every year

LUT must be renewed annually. Filing on Day 1 of the financial year ensures you never accidentally export without it and get stuck paying IGST on your first shipment.

Saves: 18% IGST on invoice value

Use PCFC instead of rupee packing credit

Pre-shipment Credit in Foreign Currency (PCFC) is typically 3–5% cheaper than rupee-denominated packing credit, as it's linked to SOFR/LIBOR + spread.

Saves: ~2–4% on working capital cost

Get ECGC cover before extending open-account terms

ECGC's SME-specific policies (NIRVIK, SEPL) insure up to 90% of invoice value against buyer default. Premium is tax-deductible. Banks also increase credit limits for insured exporters.

Protects: 90% of invoice from buyer default

Confirm your LC — don't just accept it

An unconfirmed LC protects against buyer risk but not country risk (sanctions, forex crisis). Ask your bank to add its confirmation — small fee (0.25–0.75%) for full payment guarantee.

Eliminates: country/bank default risk

Apply for Advance Authorisation for inputs

If you import any raw materials or components used in your export product, Advance Authorisation gives duty-free import. Covers basic customs duty, CVD, and often anti-dumping duty.

Saves: 10–25% on imported input cost

Use EEFC account to hedge naturally

Retain up to 100% of forex earnings in EEFC. Pay your import invoices or overseas subscriptions directly from EEFC — zero conversion cost, natural hedge against rupee movements.

Saves: 0.5–1.5% on forex conversion

Track EDPMS entries monthly, not yearly

Every unmatched EDPMS entry older than 9 months triggers RBI scrutiny. Reconcile inward remittances against shipping bills monthly — your bank's trade finance team can help.

Avoids: FEMA penalty (equal to unpaid amount)

Get a Brand Rate for Duty Drawback if volume is high

All Industry Rate (AIR) is lower than actual duty paid for most products. If your annual export value exceeds ₹5 crore, apply for Brand Rate — refund of actual customs duty paid on inputs.

Saves: 1–3% additional vs. AIR

Use MAI grant for your first trade fair

Market Access Initiative (MAI) scheme reimburses 50%–75% of stall costs at approved international trade fairs. First-time exporters from Tier-2/3 cities get priority. Apply via your export promotion council.

Saves: 50–75% of trade fair cost

Apply for EPCG before buying capital goods

Import capital goods at 0% customs duty under EPCG. Commit to 6× the duty saved in exports over 6 years. For a manufacturer importing ₹1 crore of machinery, customs duty savings = ₹15–25 lakh.

Saves: 15–25% of capital goods cost

Register on government e-marketplaces for B2B leads

DGFT's B2B portal, Indian Trade Portal (www.indiantradeportal.in), and APEDA's AGRI Exchange give free export leads and buyer databases. Most exporters don't know these exist.

Generates: Free qualified export leads

6 Government Schemes Every Exporter Should Use

Schemes with the highest impact and easiest eligibility for SMEs.

RODTEP — Remission of Duties & Taxes

0.3%–4.3% of FOB value

Refunds embedded taxes (state levies, electricity duty, mandi tax, coal cess) that aren't captured by GST or customs drawback. Replaced MEIS in January 2021. Rates are HS code-specific.

  1. Check your HS code rate in DGFT's RODTEP schedule
  2. Ensure your CHA declares RODTEP claim on the shipping bill
  3. E-scrip credit appears in your ICEGATE login within 5–7 days of LEO (Let Export Order)
  4. Transfer e-scrip to importer for customs duty payment, or sell to a broker

NIRVIK — ECGC Enhanced Cover Scheme

90% credit insurance cover

ECGC's flagship scheme for SME exporters covers 90% of principal and 90% of interest for MSMEs with export credit up to ₹50 crore. Enables banks to sanction higher credit limits.

  1. Apply through your AD bank (not directly to ECGC)
  2. Bank enrolls you in NIRVIK; premium is ~0.65% p.a. of credit limit
  3. In case of buyer default, file claim within 30 days of non-payment
  4. ECGC settles 90% of principal + 90% of interest within 30–45 days

EPCG — Export Promotion Capital Goods

0% customs duty on capital goods

Import capital goods (machinery, testing equipment, computers) at zero customs duty in exchange for an export obligation of 6× the duty saved, fulfilled within 6 years.

  1. Apply for EPCG licence at DGFT regional office before importing
  2. Import capital goods and install them
  3. Export goods produced using these capital goods (6× duty saved in 6 years)
  4. Submit export obligation discharge certificate (EODC) to DGFT on completion

MAI — Market Access Initiative

50–75% grant on market development costs

DGFT scheme that reimburses exporters and export promotion councils for international marketing activities — trade fairs, reverse buyer-seller meets, product registration abroad, market studies.

  1. Apply through your export promotion council (APEDA, EEPC, CLE, etc.)
  2. Activities must be pre-approved — apply 3 months before the event
  3. Submit utilisation certificate and bills after the activity
  4. Reimbursement processed within 60–90 days

IES — Interest Equalisation Scheme

2–5% interest rate subvention

RBI-administered scheme providing interest rate subvention (subsidy) on pre- and post-shipment rupee export credit. MSME exporters of all products get 3% subvention; merchant exporters get 2%.

  1. No separate application needed — automatically applied by your bank
  2. Ensure your bank account is tagged as an "export account" under the scheme
  3. Benefit is passed on as reduced interest rate on packing credit and post-shipment credit
  4. Bank claims reimbursement from RBI on your behalf

ODOP — One District One Product

Cluster-based export support

DGFT's initiative to develop each of India's 739 districts as a hub for one product with export potential. Provides market development assistance, branding support, common facility centres, and buyer connections for artisans and small manufacturers.

  1. Check if your district and product are covered at onedistrict.oneproduct.gov.in
  2. Register as an ODOP artisan/producer through your district collector's office
  3. Access GI tagging support, quality certification assistance, and export linkage
  4. Apply for ODOP marketing fund through the DGFT regional office

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 9-month rule for export proceeds? +
Under RBI's Master Direction on Export of Goods & Services, all export proceeds must be realised and repatriated to India within 9 months from the date of export (as evidenced by the date on the shipping bill or airway bill). For software and services exports, the 9 months runs from the invoice date. Banks track this through the EDPMS system. If payment is not received within 9 months, you must apply to your bank (AD Category-I) for an extension. Failure to realise within the permitted period attracts FEMA penalties equal to the outstanding amount.
Can I receive export payment in Indian Rupees from a foreign buyer? +
Yes, but only under specific conditions. Rupee payments from foreign buyers are permitted if: (a) the payment comes from a Vostro account of a foreign bank, or (b) the buyer is in a country that has agreed to a special rupee trade arrangement with India (currently Russia, in limited cases). Payments in rupees via PayPal, Wise, or other informal channels are NOT compliant under FEMA. Always insist on USD/EUR/GBP or other freely convertible currencies unless your CA confirms a compliant rupee arrangement.
Do I need to pay GST on exports? +
Exports are "zero-rated supplies" under the IGST Act. This means the effective GST rate on exports is 0%. However, you need to formally elect zero-rating by either: (a) Filing a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) before exporting — this allows you to export without paying IGST, and claim refund of accumulated ITC on inputs, or (b) Paying IGST at export and claiming a cash refund later. Option (a) is almost always better since it doesn't tie up your working capital. LUT must be filed annually on the GST portal before your first export of each financial year.
What is the difference between RODTEP and Duty Drawback? +
Both are export incentive schemes but they cover different taxes. Duty Drawback refunds customs duties paid on imported inputs used in the exported product. RODTEP (Remission of Duties and Taxes on Exported Products) covers embedded taxes that aren't captured by Duty Drawback — such as state-level taxes (mandi tax, electricity duty, coal cess, etc.). Both can be claimed simultaneously on the same shipping bill. Rates for Duty Drawback are at drawback.net.in; RODTEP rates are in the DGFT schedule. Both must be declared by your CHA on the shipping bill — they cannot be claimed after the fact.
What is an IEC and do I really need one? +
Yes, an Importer-Exporter Code (IEC) is mandatory for all exports from India (with very limited exceptions for personal use goods under ₹25,000). It is a 10-digit code issued by DGFT, linked to your PAN. Without an IEC, customs will not process your shipping bill and you cannot legally export. The IEC is free of cost from January 2021 (the ₹500 fee was waived). Application is online at dgft.gov.in and takes 2–3 working days. One IEC is valid for all your export businesses — you don't need a separate one for each product.
How does Razorpay handle FEMA compliance for digital service exports? +
Razorpay is an RBI-authorised Payment Aggregator operating under the FEMA framework. When you receive a payment from an overseas customer through Razorpay, the settlement comes as an inward remittance to your Indian bank account, accompanied by a Foreign Inward Remittance Advice (FIRA) document. The FIRA serves as proof of foreign exchange receipt, which you need to (a) submit to your bank for EDPMS reconciliation, (b) use for SOFTEX filing (for IT/software service exports), and (c) prove repatriation compliance to RBI. Razorpay auto-generates FIRA for each transaction, eliminating the manual documentation burden.
What is SOFTEX and when do I need to file it? +
SOFTEX (Software Export Declaration) is the mandatory declaration form for all software and IT service exports. Unlike goods exports (which use Shipping Bills), service exports use SOFTEX for FEMA compliance tracking. SOFTEX is filed through the STPI (Software Technology Parks of India) or NASSCOM e-SOFTEX portal — not through customs. You must file a SOFTEX for each export invoice above ₹25,000 (USD/foreign currency equivalent). The form captures buyer details, invoice amount, service description, and expected payment date. Your inward remittance (FIRA from Razorpay or your bank) must be matched against the filed SOFTEX.
Can a sole proprietor export? What about GST implications? +
Yes, sole proprietors can export. You need an IEC linked to your PAN. For GST, if your annual export turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh (₹10 lakh for special category states), GST registration is mandatory — even for zero-rated exports. Below this threshold, GST registration is optional but advisable, since you'll need it to file LUT and claim ITC refunds. As a sole proprietor, your EDPMS entries are linked to your PAN-based IEC and bank account. FEMA compliance obligations are the same as for any other exporter — all proceeds must be realised within 9 months.
